Abstract

Standard Scanning Transmission Electron Microscopy (STEM) is fundamentally limited by magnetic astigmatism, typically treated as a hardware aberration requiring complex multipole stigmators to correct. However, under the Topological Geon framework, this astigmatism is revealed to be the intrinsic topological shadow of the electron itself, modeled as a 4π Möbius double-loop.

By acknowledging the true geometric structure of the electron probe, we can mathematically bypass stigmator hardware entirely. Using deterministic Fourier inversion with an anisotropic Geon Point Spread Function (PSF), we strip away the Zero-Point Field (ZPF) fluid friction floor. This approach eliminates spatial blur and unlocks unprecedented sub-picometer geometric mapping across organic molecular networks, 2D continuous lattices, and complex metal oxide columns.

The Mathematics

1. Anisotropic Geon PSF

The Point Spread Function (PSF) represents the projected physical drag of a circularly polarized 4π Möbius double-loop, dominated by quadrupole structural drag (A = 0.8).

$$ PSF_{geon}(k_x, k_y) = e^{-r^2/2} \cdot \left[1 + A \cdot \cos(2\theta) \cdot e^{-r} \right] $$

2. Deterministic Fourier Inversion (Wiener Filter)

We apply this spatial frequency deconvolution to isolate the raw diffraction frame. The constant \(\epsilon = 10^{-4}\) defines the Zero-Point Field (ZPF) fluid friction floor, stabilizing the inversion.

$$ O_{true}(x,y) = \mathcal{F}^{-1} \left\{ \frac{\mathcal{F}\{I_{raw}\}}{PSF_{geon} + \epsilon} \right\} $$
